# `CompressionLevel::png()` — PNG-tuned compression, 2026-10-06

- **zenflate:** the commit that adds this revision of the file (branch `png-mode`, PR #10).
- **Host:** i265 (Intel Core Ultra 7 265K, 20 threads, native Linux, no AVX-512). Shared box; load average 1–6 during runs.
- **Build:** release, safe (no `unchecked`), no `-C target-cpu=native`.
- **Harness:** `benchmarks/harnesses/png-mode/` (standalone package). Validation corpus tooling and analysis scripts: `benchmarks/harnesses/png-mode/validation/`.
- **Comparators:** fdeflate `main` at `a713d02` (unreleased 0.4 compressor), zlib-rs 0.6.6.

## The ladder

| Effort | Encoder |
|---|---|
| `png(1)` | Ultra-fast: literals + zero runs, one Huffman table per stream (counted from the whole input up to 64 KiB, sampled above) |
| `png(2)` | Runs only (distance-1 matches), exact Huffman per 128 KiB block |
| `png(3)` | + hashed repeats of 8+ bytes (single-entry table) |
| `png(4)`–`png(5)` | + hashed repeats of 5+ bytes (single-entry table) |
| `png(6)`–`png(12)` | hash chains of depth 2 → 64 |
| `png(13..)` | identical to `new(13..)` |

Every hash effort also parses each block runs-only and emits the cheaper parse (the runs-only guard), and the skip-ahead step is capped at 256 bytes.

At native sizes with the adaptive filter, measured on 146 held-out images: `png(1)` runs at 2,780 MiB/s (fdeflate ultra-fast: 1,273 MiB/s at a worse ratio). `png(3)` is 33% faster than fdeflate L1 at a 0.4% larger size, `png(4)` beats fdeflate L2 on both axes, and `png(7)` beats fdeflate L3 on both axes. `new(1)` (315 MiB/s, 2.438) is dominated by `png(4)`.

## Held-out validation

### Method

- **Corpus:** imazen-26 cluster representatives (`manifests/imazen26_representatives_K300_2026-06-14.tsv`, k-means picks with crop labels) in the validate and test splits, minus the 35 images the first ladder was tuned on: 150 (image, crop) units, 96 validate + 54 test, 18 of 21 categories. Selection: `validation/selection.tsv`.
- **Sizes:** each unit's crop region (`c50`/`c25` = square of half/quarter the short side, per imazen-26's `crop_rect`) at three sizes:
  - *native:* the crop at its own resolution, center-cropped to at most 4 MP, no resampling. 146 units decode (4 bilevel patent scans come out 1-bit and are skipped); median 512×512, 33 above 1 MP.
  - *256×256* and *64×64:* Lanczos downscales of the crop region (thumbnails); no upscaling, so 134 units qualify for 256×256 and 150 for 64×64.
- **Transforms:** per-row adaptive filter (minimum sum of absolute residuals) and filter None.
- **Statistics:** for each step from level *n−1* to *n*, the per-image change in compressed size, `ln(size_n / size_n-1)`, reported as mean ± 95% CI (1.96·SD/√n), its SD, how many images got larger, and the largest increase. Ratio is total input / total output; MiB/s is total input / summed per-image median times.
- **Timing:** interleaved: each repetition round runs every codec once per image (5, 11 and 21 rounds for native, 256 and 64), median per image. Compressors and output buffers are reused across calls.
- **Verification:** every output is decoded by zenflate and compared byte for byte before timing.
- **Data:** aggregates in `png_mode_ladder_validation_2026-10-06_{native,256px,64px}.csv`. Per-image sizes and times (not committed, 60–70 KB each compressed): host i265, `~/data/zenflate/png-mode-2026-10-06/ladder-validation/v4-{native,256,64}.log.xz`, sha256 in that directory's `SHA256SUMS` (`b28c6fd8…`, `6f7d1769…`, `389e47ff…`). The `v3-*` logs there measured the first table rule (fixed table under 64 KiB); only `png(1)` on inputs under 64 KiB changed between them. The candidate sweeps that chose the ladder (`v2-*`, `cap-*`, `gap-*`, `mm-native`) are in the same directory.

### Boundaries

Three steps change the algorithm, and `monotonicity_fallback()` covers each:

- **`png(1)` → `png(2)`** (one table per stream → exact Huffman per block): at native sizes and 256×256, 2–5 images larger by up to 1.4% filtered and none unfiltered. At 64×64, where `png(1)` counts its table over the whole input, 24 of 150 images are larger by up to 4.5% filtered and one by 0.27% unfiltered. Fallback `png(2)` → `png(1)`.
- **`png(3)` → `png(4)`** (min match 8 → 5): the mean improves 0.45–1.3% filtered and 3–3.4% unfiltered, but 27–38 images get larger, by up to 6.5% filtered. Short matches preempt longer ones in a greedy parse. Fallback `png(4..=12)` → `png(3)`.
- **`png(12)` → `png(13)`** (PNG parser → `new()`'s lazy parser): no average gain filtered at native sizes (+0.25% ± 0.80%), but 85 of 146 images get larger (by up to 15%) while the rest get smaller, and one unfiltered image gets 268% larger. The two parsers win on different images. Fallback `png(13..=17)` → `png(12)`.

Inside the groups, every filtered step is significant in every bucket (the 95% CI excludes 0), and at most 15 images get larger at any step; the worst filtered increase is 2.05%. Unfiltered, `png(8)` and `png(9)` are not significant at native and 256×256 sizes, because one plot (7095) swings by +43% to +86% between greedy levels: greedy parsers land between 2.7 and 6.4 KB on it depending on small parameter changes, `new(10)` at 4.6 KB, and `new(15)`'s lazy parser at 2.6 KB.

### What the held-out set changed

The first ladder (below) was tuned on 35 images. On the held-out set:

1. **Skip-ahead could run away.** The step `1 + gap >> skip_shift` grows until a match or a block start, so after a long matchless stretch the parser jumped kilobytes at a time across compressible data. A manuscript scan at skip shift 5 came out 32% larger than at shifts 4 and 6. Capping the step at 256 fixes it (141,808 → 105,775 bytes) at a speed cost within noise for most settings and 7% at worst; caps of 16–64 help unfiltered data a little more at 5–24% speed.
2. **A min-match-8 level fills most of the runs-only → hash gap.** Runs-only is 1,165 MiB/s and min match 5 is 356 MiB/s. Min match 8 sits at 519 MiB/s and 10.4% smaller than runs-only with no image larger. Stepping through min match 7 and 6 instead gave mean gains of 0.1–0.9% per step but made 27–42 of 146 images larger at each step (up to 6%), so the ladder makes one switch, 8 → 5, with a fallback.
3. **Long-match-only levels without the runs-only guard don't work.** Accepting only hashed matches of 12–24+ bytes and skipping the guard still made 34–48 of 146 images larger than runs-only (up to 12%): any distance code beyond 1 lengthens the run codes on flat art.
4. **The deep chain levels were dominated by `new(13..)`.** The first ladder's `png(13..14)` (chain depth 128–258) were slower than `new(13..14)` at equal or worse ratio at native sizes, and clearly worse at 256×256, 64×64 and unfiltered. The ladder now ends at 12.
5. **Ultra-fast tables on small inputs.** At 64×64, a table built with a code for every symbol ran at 984 MiB/s against 1,544 MiB/s for fdeflate's fixed table, so the first version used the fixed table below 64 KiB. A later revision counts small inputs instead and codes only the symbols that can occur: 12–14% smaller than the fixed table at a few microseconds per image (see [Tables for `png(1)`](#tables-for-png1)). Above 64 KiB the sampled table is 7–9% smaller than the fixed one at 0–10% lower speed.
6. **Per-block ultra tables were rejected.** Sampling a table per 256 KiB block was 0.2% smaller on average (not significant) but made one plot 44% larger: blocks of mostly runs give tables dominated by the "every symbol needs a code" prior.

## Ultra-fast mode (`png(1)`)

The token model comes from fdeflate's ultra-fast mode; the encoder is new. Ratio @ MiB/s on the held-out set, adaptive filter (stored mode is memcpy plus Adler-32 and framing, the practical ceiling):

| Size | `png(1)` | fdeflate ultra-fast | zenflate stored (`new(0)`) |
|---|---|---|---|
| native | 2.237 @ 2,780 MiB/s | 2.087 @ 1,273 | 1.0 @ 12,477 |
| 256×256 | 2.504 @ 2,222 | 2.295 @ 1,372 | 1.0 @ 28,205 |
| 64×64 | 2.221 @ 950 | 2.018 @ 1,155 | 1.0 @ 32,622 |

Blocks that would come out larger than stored are rewritten as stored blocks, compared in bits (an earlier revision compared whole bytes, so a block within two bytes of its stored size could overrun `zlib_compress_bound`, which is tight below 5,000 bytes of input).

Literal emission dominates (about 8 instructions per byte, half lookup, half bit-writing). Three bit-writer and table-layout variants measured no faster, and a 128-bit single-flush writer was slower. On the 35 tuning crops at 1 MP, photo-like content ran at about 2.0–2.2 GB/s and screenshots and documents (mostly zero runs) at 3.7–5.7 GB/s.

## Tables for `png(1)`

`png(1)` codes the whole stream with one Huffman table. Up to 64 KiB of input, the table comes from the token counts of the whole input; longer inputs count evenly spaced 4 KiB segments covering about 1/16 of it. The first version used fdeflate's fixed table below 64 KiB instead.

### Can a better fixed table replace counting?

- **Data:** training: the imazen-26 K500 cluster representatives in the train split (254 crops of 212 images, disjoint from the held-out set; `tables/make_train_selection.py`). Held-out: the 150 validation units above. Each at 19 sizes from 24 to 1024 px plus native, as RGB and as gray (`tables/make_dense.py`): 8,908 training and 5,076 held-out streams, adaptive filter.
- **Model:** per stream, header bits plus code bits for its token histogram (tables by package-merge, codes up to 12 bits). These are modeled sizes, not encoder output. Output: `png_ultra_table_model_2026-10-06.log`.
- **Reproduce** (from `benchmarks/harnesses/png-mode/`; the trainer needs numpy):

  ```sh
  python3 -I tables/make_train_selection.py ~/work/zen/imazen-26 > train_selection.tsv
  python3 -I tables/make_dense.py train_selection.tsv src train
  python3 -I tables/make_dense.py validation/selection.tsv src holdout
  cargo run --release --bin token_hist -- train > hist_train.tsv
  cargo run --release --bin token_hist -- holdout > hist_holdout.tsv
  python3 tables/train_tables.py hist_train.tsv hist_holdout.tsv
  ```

| Table | Size |
|---|---|
| Static Huffman block (no header) | +51.2% |
| One table trained on the training streams | −0.9% |

Both 256×256 sizes are above 64 KiB, so both counted columns sample there; that is also what the first version did. Giving every symbol a code, the rule a sample needs, costs up to 12% on 24×24 images, because the header then carries a code length for every absent literal. A whole-input count is exact for nonzero bytes, which are always literals, so absent ones get no code. Literal 0, end-of-block and the length symbols keep a prior, because how zero runs split into literal 0s and lengths depends on block boundaries and the run threshold.

Up to 128×128 the counted table takes 1.7–5.5× the fixed table's time (2–13 µs more per image). `png(2)` takes 0–23% longer than that and compresses 1.2–4.4% smaller.

### Rejected

Measured like the table above, plus the native and 1 MP held-out sets at 7 rounds.

- **Shorter sample segments.** At native sizes, 512-byte segments cost 0.75% and 256-byte segments 6.2% (mean per image): short segments cut long zero runs and skew the length codes. 1–2 KiB tie 4 KiB.
- **Counting the whole input above 64 KiB.** 0.8% smaller in aggregate (3.9% mean per image) at native sizes, at 41% lower throughput.
- **Starting zero runs at one or two trailing zero bytes of a chunk** (fdeflate starts at one; `png(1)` needs four). 0.24% smaller in aggregate at native sizes, at 18% lower throughput: more chunks take the per-byte path.
- **A codebook of fixed tables.** No counting pass, but it recovers only about half the gain (above).

## Earlier results (first ladder, superseded)

The tables in `png_mode_2026-10-06_{1mp,256px,64px}.csv` measure the first ladder (efforts 1–14, no step cap, runs-only at efforts 1–2) on the 35 tuning crops, timed sequentially. They are kept for provenance; the held-out validation above supersedes them.

### Tuning on the first 35 images

1. **First cut: fdeflate's parsers ported onto zenflate's block writer.** Runs first, then an 8-byte hash, then skip-ahead. It beat fdeflate L1 and zenflate `new(1)` on aggregate ratio and speed.
2. **Aggregate Pareto picks hid large per-image inversions.**
   - Moving from runs-only to hashing made 15 of 35 images up to 9.1% larger.
   - Changing min match mid-ladder (6 → 5) made 20 of 35 images up to 1.9% larger.
3. **A greedy bit-cost model (reject matches dearer than their literals) did not fix the runs-only → hash inversion.** It also cost 15–25% speed, and its order-0 first-block estimate hurt small inputs. It was removed.
4. **The runs-only → hash inversion is flat-colour clipart.** Far LZ77 matches split runs and lengthen the distance-1 code, so runs alone win by up to 10%. Estimating the runs-only cost from the main parse (demoting hashed matches to literals) misses it, because those matches swallow run starts.
5. **What fixed it:** parse each block runs-only as well and emit the cheaper parse. To keep this affordable, blocks are fixed at 128 KiB so every effort splits identically, and the comparison pauses for 3 blocks after runs-only loses by more than 1/8 (photo content).
6. **Ladder rules:** skip shift and chain depth never decrease, min match stays at 5 for every hash effort, and `png(3)` uses `png(2)`'s skip shift, so its guard reproduces `png(2)`'s parse.
7. **Chain-walk cost at small sizes:** byte-at-a-time backward extension for every candidate made deep chains 3–4× slower per byte on 256×256 crops. Forward-first extension with a length bound, plus 8-byte backward compares, fixed it.

## Where `png()` wins and where it doesn't

- **Filtered input:** `png(1..=12)` are on the ratio/speed Pareto front against the measured `new()` efforts and fdeflate at native sizes; at 256×256 `new(12..)` overtakes from about 150 MiB/s, at 64×64 `new(10..)` from about 180 MiB/s.
- **Unfiltered input:** `new(10..)` compresses better than `png(10..=12)` at similar speed, because it takes 3–4 byte matches the PNG parser skips.
- **Small inputs:** at 64×64 `png(1)` counts its table: 9% smaller output than fdeflate ultra-fast at 82% of its speed, and as fast as `png(2)`, which compresses 1.7% smaller on average (8% unfiltered). Every level up to `png(17)` takes under 0.1 ms per 64×64 image, so on small images the effort choice costs microseconds. Whole zenpng encodes take far longer there (strategy screening, refine, brute force); zenpng effort timing by image size and a proposed effort floor for small images: imazen/zenpng#23.
